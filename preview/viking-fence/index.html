Austin’s 6-foot line

Their own regulations page

Viking publishes the City of Austin rule in plain words: no permit for a fence 6 feet or lower, a permit generally required above that, with exceptions for pools and sloping ground — and an HOA may ask for something different again.

Straight answers, from their own pages.

Warranty, published in full

One year on workmanship and material, with defects in that period replaced at no cost. Pre-manufactured ornamental iron carries the manufacturer’s own 5 to 20 year term. Gate operators and access control: defects repaired without labour charge in year one, parts still covered afterwards while the factory warranty runs. Western red cedar is not warranted against termites, and shifting soil is excluded.

Their own cedar mill

Viking says it is the only fence company in Austin with its own remanufacturing facility, resawing and custom cutting to specification, with picket grades produced to its own standard — Select Tight Knot, Residential 2/Btr, and an import cedar.

Memberships

Their warranty certificate lists membership of the Better Business Bureau in Austin, Dallas and Fort Worth, and of the American Fence Association.

Where the work happens

Residential installation and repair across more than thirty Greater Austin towns, from Bastrop and Buda to Pflugerville, Round Rock and Wimberley. Commercial installation in Austin, San Antonio, Waco, Dallas/Ft. Worth and Houston. Rentals statewide from four branches.

Beyond the fence line

Driveway and automatic gates, access control, perimeter security, iron railings, government fencing, trash and generator enclosures, water barricades, portable sanitation and storage containers.

Permits in Austin

Viking’s own Austin fence regulations page says a property owner does not need a permit for a fence 6 feet or lower, and that fences taller than 6 feet generally do require one. Viking’s Austin fence regulations page also notes exceptions for pools and sloping ground, says residential variances are not hard to acquire and that commercial properties have more flexibility, and warns that an HOA may set requirements of its own. Most surrounding cities, but not all, have adopted the City of Austin fence code.
