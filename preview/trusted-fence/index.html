● WHAT THEY BUILD

Six materials, and what each one is actually for.

Grouped the way their own installation pages are. Every sentence below is theirs.

01

Wood (cedar)

CEDAR · 6 named styles

Western red cedar, the Wasatch classic. They quote stain-and-seal up front, because unsealed cedar grays out in about two summers at this altitude.

02

Vinyl

VINYL · 3 named styles

Mostly full-height tongue-and-groove privacy: no gaps, no visible fasteners, white or almond. They install only UV-inhibited panels warrantied against fading.

03

Chain link

CHAIN LINK · 5 named styles

Black, classic silver, or vinyl-coated in black or green. Heights run from 4-foot yard fences to 12-foot commercial lines, tensioned drum-tight against canyon wind.

04

Aluminum

ALUMINUM · 3 named styles

Powder-coated and rackable, so it follows your slope without cutting panels. Black is the classic; bronze and white suit lighter homes.

05

Ornamental iron & steel

IRON · 3 named styles

Welded steel pickets: the fence you buy once. Custom heights for east-bench deer pressure, and estate gates to match. Every panel powder-coated.

06

Composite

COMPOSITE · 2 named styles

SimTek, Trex and Avimore: the woodgrain look without rot or peeling, with enough mass to dampen road noise. Costs more per foot than cedar or vinyl up front.

● BEFORE YOU CALL

Straight answers, from their own site.

No trip fee, ever

Their FAQ: “No. Estimates are free and there’s no trip fee. We walk your property line, measure it, talk through materials, and put a number in writing. You pay nothing unless you decide to build.”

Half down, the rest when it’s right

Half the payment to start, the rest due only when the work is done and you are happy with it. They are licensed and insured throughout.

Posts two feet deep

Every post is set two feet deep in concrete, not tamped dirt, and gate posts go deeper than the line. They say they set posts deeper here than the manual calls for, because of freeze-thaw and canyon wind.

How fast

They respond to every call and form the same day. Once on site, most residential fences go up in one to three days depending on footage and material.

HOA rules

Their FAQ: tell them your HOA and they spec the fence to match its rules, and give you the drawings and material sheets you need for approval before anything is ordered.

Winter work

Usually yes. They set posts two feet deep in concrete year-round; deep frozen ground can occasionally push a start date, and they say they will tell you straight instead of doing it wrong.

Permits in Salt Lake City

Trusted Fence: “Salt Lake City requires a permit from Building Services for fence installation, and the rules depend on height, material, and where the fence sits on your lot. Generally, fences can be 4 feet tall in front yards and 6 feet tall along the sides and back, with a lower limit near corner intersections.” They add that homeowners can generally apply for their own primary residence, while rental and commercial properties need a licensed contractor, and that towns like Sandy, Draper and Millcreek set their own rules. They check the requirements for your address before they build.
