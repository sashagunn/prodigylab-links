What's holding your business back

Six places where established businesses lose money quietly

We work across all six. Some we solve directly. For others we bring in a specialist provider we've vetted — and we tell you which is which before you commit to anything.

01 — Payments

Your processor stopped working for you

Accounts suspended, funds held, chargebacks climbing, or a vertical your current provider quietly doesn't want.

  • Account closed or under review
  • High chargeback ratio
  • Subscription or high-risk billing
  • Needs redundancy or alternative rails
02 — Banking

Banking that doesn't fit how you operate

Especially for US companies with founders abroad, or businesses whose bank no longer matches their model.

  • Account frozen or closed
  • Foreign founder, US entity
  • Opening business banking from abroad
  • Need alternative infrastructure
03 — Growth

Traffic and spend that don't turn into revenue

The acquisition side works on paper and fails in practice — usually in one specific place, not everywhere.

  • Clicks without orders
  • Rising cost per customer
  • Weak conversion at a known step
04 — Operations

Work that depends on someone remembering

Leads in four inboxes, a CRM nobody trusts, reporting assembled by hand on Sunday night.

  • Fragmented systems
  • Manual, repeatable processes
  • No single owner for the digital side
05 — Infrastructure

Functions too small to hire for, too costly to ignore

Fulfilment, payroll, security, business tooling — necessary, recurring, and rarely worth a full salary.

  • Fulfilment and 3PL
  • Payroll and HR
  • Security and compliance
06 — Digital presence

The storefront your buyers actually judge you on

Website and e-commerce infrastructure: the part we have built for years and still build ourselves.

  • Site or store rebuild
  • Conversion and checkout
  • E-commerce infrastructure

Who we are

An operating company, not a directory of links

Prodigy LAB has spent years building and fixing the systems businesses sell through — stores, websites, advertising, conversion, customer conversations. That work continues, and it's the reason we can tell an infrastructure problem from a marketing one.

Advisory grew out of it. Clients kept arriving with problems that weren't web problems at all — a processor that dropped them, a bank that closed an account, a function nobody owned. Answering only the part that fit our service list wasn't useful to them.

What we don't do. We are not a payment processor and not a bank. We don't approve merchant accounts or open bank accounts, and we can't guarantee that a provider will. We don't provide legal, tax, investment or other regulated financial advice, and we don't handle company formation, visas or immigration.

Where a specialist is required, that's exactly what we'll tell you.
