What both cases have in common

The owner misdiagnosed it

In both, the thing about to be bought was not the thing that was broken. The audit is what caught the difference.

The fix was smaller than expected

Neither needed everything rebuilt. Finding the actual constraint is most of the work.

The proof came fast or not at all

When the constraint is the right one, the change shows up in days, not quarters. When nothing moves in a month, the diagnosis was wrong and it gets said out loud.
